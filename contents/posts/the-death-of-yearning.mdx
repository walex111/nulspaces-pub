Yearning is an ancient technology. It is the quiet, inherent frequency of the human soul, a state of being that requires distance to exist.

When the men of old looked upon a subject of devotion, they looked with the eyes of love; a gaze that sought to honor, to wait, and to understand. It was a tension held with discipline, a recognition that the most beautiful things are not those we can grab, but those we can barely reach.

Today, the horizon has collapsed.

In its place, I see only the frantic pulse of lust, consuming fire that mistakes possession for connection. We have become a culture of the instant, where the space between wanting and having is treated as a flaw rather than a sanctuary.

We have traded the slow, sacred ache of yearning for the hollow efficiency of the hunt. In doing so, we have lost the ability to truly see one another. We see only what we can take.
